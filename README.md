## What SUI is (brief, because you mostly want to buy it)

SUI is the native token of the Sui blockchain, a Layer 1 network built by Mysten Labs — a team that came out of Meta's discontinued Diem project. Sui uses an object-centric data model and the Move programming language, which allows parallel transaction processing. In plain terms, it's designed for high throughput and low fees, and SUI itself is used for gas, staking, and governance on the network.

The token has a total supply of 10 billion, with a circulating portion that unlocks gradually according to a published vesting schedule. That unlock schedule matters if you're paying attention to price action, but it doesn't change where or how you buy — so we'll leave the tokenomics deep dive for another time.

## Storing SUI after you buy it

Once you own SUI, you have two broad choices: leave it on the exchange where you bought it, or move it to a wallet you control.

**Leaving it on the exchange** is convenient and lets you stake, trade, or sell quickly. The trade-off is that you're relying on the exchange's security and policies. OKX publishes Proof of Reserves showing user assets are backed 1:1, which is more transparency than most exchanges offer — but it's still custodial storage, meaning you don't hold the keys.

**Moving SUI to your own wallet** gives you full control and is the standard recommendation for longer-term holdings. Wallet options that support the Sui network include:

- **Sui Wallet** — the official browser extension and mobile wallet from the Sui team
- **OKX Wallet** — multi-chain wallet with a Sui network option; works as a browser extension and inside the OKX app
- **Backpack** — multi-chain wallet that supports Sui
- **Suiet, Martian, Nightly, Slush** — third-party Sui-compatible wallets
- **Ledger** — hardware wallet with Sui support through third-party integrations

The transfer process is simple: install the wallet, back up the recovery phrase somewhere safe (not on a cloud service in plain text), copy your Sui address, and withdraw from the exchange to that address. Double-check the address before confirming — Sui addresses are long and a single wrong character means lost funds.

One thing that trips people up: SUI is a native token on its own chain, not an ERC-20 token. You need a wallet that specifically supports the Sui network, and when withdrawing from an exchange you need to select Sui as the network. Sending SUI to an Ethereum-only wallet will result in a failed or stuck transaction.

## Common questions about buying SUI

**Is SUI available in the U.S.?**

Yes, but not on every exchange. Coinbase, Kraken, and Robinhood list SUI for U.S. users. OKX operates a U.S.-focused platform with SUI spot pairs in supported states. Binance.com is not available in the U.S.; Binance.US has a more limited selection that doesn't always include SUI. Check the specific platform's supported regions before signing up.

**Do I need to complete KYC to buy SUI?**

On any regulated centralized exchange, yes. P2P platforms and some DEX aggregators allow purchases with less verification, but they come with higher counterparty risk and aren't worth the trade-off for most buyers. KYC on a reputable exchange typically takes anywhere from a few minutes to a couple of days depending on document load.

**What's the minimum I can buy?**

It depends on the exchange and pair. On OKX's spot market, the minimum order size for SUI/USDT is 1 SUI, which at current prices is under a dollar. Express Buy and card purchases sometimes have a higher minimum (often $10–$20 equivalent) because of card processing fees.

**Can I buy SUI with a credit card?**

Yes, on most major exchanges including OKX, Coinbase, and Binance where supported. Card purchases are fast but carry a processing fee — typically 2–4% on top of the exchange's trading fee — so bank transfer or crypto deposit is cheaper if you can wait.

**Should I stake SUI after buying?**

Staking can earn you additional SUI over time, but it locks your tokens for the staking period (unless you use a flexible staking product). If you think you might need to sell quickly, don't stake the portion you want liquid. If you're holding long term, staking the bulk of your position is reasonable.

**Is SUI the same as Sui?**

"Sui" is the blockchain and the project. "SUI" (all caps) is the token ticker. People use them interchangeably in casual conversation, but when you're searching for a trading pair on an exchange, look for **SUI**.

## A few things worth not skipping

Whatever exchange you choose, a couple of habits will save you money and headaches:

- **Compare the effective price, not just the fee rate.** A 0.10% fee with a tight spread can beat a 0% fee with a wide spread. Check the actual fill price against a price aggregator like CoinMarketCap or CoinGecko before confirming large orders.
- **Enable two-factor authentication.** Not optional. Use an authenticator app, not SMS, if the platform supports it.
- **Test withdrawals with a small amount first.** Before moving your entire SUI balance to a new wallet, send 1 SUI to confirm the address works and the wallet receives it correctly.
- **Keep records for taxes.** In most jurisdictions, buying and later selling crypto is a taxable event. Exchanges export transaction histories — download them periodically rather than scrambling at tax time.
- **Don't keep more on an exchange than you need for trading.** Exchanges get hacked, freeze accounts, or change withdrawal policies. Long-term holdings belong in a wallet you control.

None of this is specific to SUI, but it's the boring stuff that actually determines whether your purchase goes smoothly or turns into a support ticket.
